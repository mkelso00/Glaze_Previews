Where the money is leaking, and what to fix before Q4

The brand is working. Traffic is strong at 335,420 sessions a quarter, the returning customer rate is 43.2%, average product ratings sit between 4.75 and 5.0, and the blog ranks well enough to be six of the top twenty entry points. The problem is the shop itself: 80% of visits are on mobile, and mobile is where the funnel breaks. Nov and Dec were 25.3% of last year's revenue, so the window to land this work is roughly ten weeks.

03  /  Reviews and social proof

Customers love the products. You are barely collecting the proof

The store runs Judge.me. Average ratings sit between 4.75 and 5.0 across your best sellers, which is excellent. The problem is volume and placement: some products collect reviews at ten times the rate of others, and the ones with the thinnest proof are often the ones selling hardest.

The clearest example

The Lilac Dainty Wrap Rosary Bracelet sold 167 times in the last 90 days and holds a perfect 5.0 rating from just 7 reviews. Every one of those buyers loved it and almost none of them were successfully asked. That is not a product problem, it is a request flow problem.

Fix the collection engine

  • Set review request timing by product type. Jewellery needs a week of wear before anyone has an opinion. A wall plaque is judged the day it is hung. One global delay serves neither.
  • Turn on second and third reminders. Most stores collect over half their reviews on reminder two.
  • Add SMS review requests for customers who have opted in. Response rates run well above email.
  • Incentivise photos specifically, not reviews generally. Your existing photo reviews are the best asset on the site.
  • Import any reviews sitting in the old Shopify Product Reviews app before it is fully retired.

Then use what you collect

  • Move the rating summary next to the price. Reviews currently start about halfway down the product page, which on mobile is a long way past the buy decision.
  • Surface customer photos on collection pages and in email. Devotional purchases are settled by seeing the piece in a real home.
  • Turn on Judge.me Q&A. Buyers of a $179 necklace ask about chain length, plating and provenance, and answering publicly once saves the support ticket every time.
  • Put the rating into meta titles for your strongest products and into paid ad copy. A 4.94 from 255 reviews is worth showing before the click.
  • Pull review language into product copy. Your customers describe these pieces better than a copywriter will.

One technical thing to clean up

Your products carry both Judge.me metafields and the legacy reviews.rating metafields from the old Shopify Product Reviews app. Two sources of truth for the same star rating is how stores end up with mismatched counts, or empty stars rendering on pages with no reviews. Decide which is authoritative, migrate the rest, and remove the orphaned metafields.

Social traffic deserves its own pages

64,402 sessions a quarter arrive from social and convert at 0.98%, well under the 1.63% site average. That is a message match problem, not an audience problem. Traffic from a reel about a home blessing should land on a page about home blessings with the pieces on it, not on the homepage or a bare product page. One product supports a dozen pages if you vary the angle rather than the product.

Colour variants are being sold as separate products

The Regina Caeli range exists as six separate products, ring, earrings and necklace in two colourways. The Sacred Heart and St. Benedict tassels are split the same way, as are the boy and girl baptism shells. Each split divides the reviews, the ranking signals and the customer's attention across near-identical pages. Consolidating into variants gives you fewer, stronger pages with pooled social proof.

Credit where it is due

  • Product meta descriptions are specific, human and keyword aware. That is unusual on a 1,000 product catalogue.
  • Shopify category metafields are populated: jewellery type, material, colour, age group, target gender. Your Google Shopping feed will be better for it than most competitors'.
  • Judge.me is emitting review structured data, so rich result eligibility is already in place.
  • Collection copy, where it exists, is long and genuinely useful.
  • Server response is 142 ms. Crawl budget is not the problem.

Where to grow next

  • Build topic clusters. You have strong individual posts on saints, medals and home blessings but no hub pages tying them together and passing authority to collections.
  • Link from every blog post to the collection it should feed. Right now those links are generic.
  • Devotion-led collection pages are an open goal: Miraculous Medal, Sacred Heart, St. Benedict, Our Lady of Guadalupe. People search the devotion, not the product type.
  • Occasion pages have seasonal search demand you already have collections for: baptism, first communion, confirmation, marriage.
  • Verify rich results are actually showing in Search Console rather than assuming the markup is enough.

The site is carrying far too much JavaScript

Both pages I tested paint quickly and then sit there unresponsive. That is app sprawl, not hosting: your server responds in 142 ms on desktop, which is excellent. The weight is all front end.

What is actually wrong

  • 212 separate JavaScript files on one product page, 9.42 MB uncompressed. That is an app audit waiting to happen.
  • 3.2 seconds of JavaScript execution and 20 long main thread tasks on mobile.
  • A 2,448 element DOM on the product page.
  • 31 images with no width and height set. Your layout shift score is fine today, which means you are lucky, not safe.
  • Nine font files. Two weights of two families is plenty.

How to fix it without a rebuild

  • Export the app list and put a revenue number next to every one. On Plus, most stores carry three or four apps nobody has opened in a year.
  • Check for orphaned scripts from uninstalled apps still loading from the theme and from Custom Pixels.
  • Defer everything not needed for first render: reviews, chat, and any personalisation widget.
  • Set explicit width and height on images, and preload the hero image so LCP stops waiting.
  • Fix the cache policy, worth 206 KB on repeat visits, and self host the fonts you keep.
07  /  Urgent, separate from the funnel

Someone is running a copy of your store

A clone site is ranking on your brand name

Searching your brand returns houseofjoppacollection.shop, which uses your exact title tag, House of Joppa: #1 Rated Catholic & Religious Store in the USA. It sells what look like your products at 70% off, roughly $9 to $26, with no company details, no contact address and no policies. A second lookalike, houseofjoppa.shop, appears in search results but no longer resolves.

Meanwhile you are running effectively no paid search, 17 sessions in 90 days, so your brand name is undefended in the one place this site is competing with you.

  • File a trademark and copyright complaint with the registrar and the host today. Your product photography is your strongest lever.
  • Submit a Google removal request and report the site through Search Console.
  • Turn on a small brand defence campaign on Google Ads. At your traffic levels the cost is trivial and it puts you above the imposter.
  • Add a short buying-from-us note to your FAQ so customers who get burned can tell the difference and come back to you rather than assume it was you.

I have not test-purchased from that site, so I cannot say whether it ships counterfeit goods or nothing at all. The chargeback and brand damage risk is the same either way.

09  /  Method and gaps

How I got here, and what I could not see

Data sources. Store analytics, catalogue and metadata came from the House of Joppa Shopify admin, covering 29 May to 26 August 2026. Performance came from two GTmetrix tests run from San Antonio in Chrome: the homepage on desktop with no throttling, and the hero product page on a simulated Pixel 8 at 10.24 Mbps with 50 ms latency. Those two tests are not like for like, so read each against its own target rather than against each other. Page content came from fetching the live pages. SEO metadata figures come from a full audit of all 64 collections and a 50 product sample.

Where the estimates are soft. Every money figure is a modelled range, not a forecast. They assume traffic holds and that a fixed step performs like the benchmark named beside it. They also overlap: a conversion fix and an AOV fix touch the same orders, so do not add the ranges together. A fair combined expectation for the whole programme, landed well, is somewhere in the region of $250,000 to $500,000 of additional annual revenue.

What I could not check. No access to Google Analytics or Search Console, so there is no keyword, ranking, impression or bounce data beyond what Shopify records. No app list, because the connection lacks that permission, which is why the app cull is a recommendation rather than a named list. I could not see a populated cart, so a free shipping bar may appear once items are added. I did not test the checkout itself, and on Plus that deserves its own pass. Email platform configuration and Judge.me settings were inferred from the storefront and metafields, not inspected directly.

One thing worth watching. August 2026 is tracking at $6,354 a day against $6,708 a day in August 2025, down about 5%. One partial month is not a trend and the comparison is crude, but it deserves a proper year on year look before Q4 planning is signed off.
