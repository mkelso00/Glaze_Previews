Open decisions

Six things that change the map

Each of these moves URLs or templates, so they are worth settling before wireframes are signed off.

1. Booking engine

AnyRoad, FareHarbor or Shopify decides whether experiences live at /products/… (Shopify) or as Shopify pages with an embedded widget.

Recommendation: keep every experience as a Shopify page whichever engine wins, so the search-friendly content and URLs belong to Church of Oak and survive a change of supplier. This matches section 13.3 of the booking spec.

2. Where batch data lives

Metaobjects in Shopify give clean URLs at /pages/batch/{number}. A live NetSuite lookup would need an app proxy URL such as /apps/trace/{number}.

Recommendation: sync NetSuite into Shopify metaobjects and keep the /pages/batch/ pattern. NFC tags printed now will point at it for years, so the URL must never change.

3. Batch pages in Google

Hundreds of near-identical batch pages can read as thin content and dilute the site.

Recommendation: noindex individual batch pages and index the Trace hub and the Grain page, which carry the story. Batch pages stay public, as agreed on 12 November, since an NFC scan cannot prove purchase.

4. Membership name and scope

"Join the congregation" already runs on the holding site. Is the Congregation a free list, or a members tier with its own area and benefits?

Recommendation: launch as a free list at P1, add a tagged members area at P4 once there is something to give members (priority ballots, member events).

5. Countries sold to

Selling to the UK, EU or US changes the structure. Shopify Markets adds subfolders such as /en-gb/ and hreflang tags, and US alcohol shipping rules are state by state.

Recommendation: launch Ireland first with one domain, plan subfolders for the UK and EU at P2, and treat the US as a distributor link rather than a direct shop.

6. The Lever Room

The founders' cask programme is private. It can be a hidden page with a password field, or a page only shown to customers tagged as founders.

Recommendation: customer-tag gating, noindex, no links from the public site. Cask payments run through Shopify invoice links as agreed on 12 November.

Shopify rules this map follows

Why the URLs look the way they do

  • Shopify fixes the folder for each object: /pages/, /products/, /collections/, /blogs/, /policies/. Clean top-level URLs such as /visit are not possible, so the handle after the folder carries the keyword.
  • Metaobject entries with web pages switched on publish at /pages/{type}/{handle}. That is how batches, experiences detail and team profiles can get their own pages without an app.
  • Legal policies set in Settings publish at /policies/… automatically. Booking terms and ballot terms are not standard policies, so they are normal pages.
  • The Journal uses one blog with tags for seasons and topics, rather than several blogs, so every story shares one URL folder and builds authority in one place.
  • New customer accounts sit on Shopify's own account pages. Bookings and guest passes appear there only if the chosen booking engine has a customer account extension.
  • The old WordPress URLs were redirected at the holding-site stage. Any page in this map that replaces a former WordPress page should reuse that redirect target.
