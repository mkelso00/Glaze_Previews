Search intent

"Home gym equipment" is a research query, not a buying one. The page earns the click by answering the questions that come before the purchase, which is also what gives it something to rank on beyond a product grid.

Place before product

Garage, spare room and office are the three ways people describe this to themselves. Naming them lets the visitor self-identify in one click, and each route carries different constraints worth spelling out.

The planner is the hook

Ceiling height is the single constraint that catches people out, and nobody searches for it. Asking for it up front prevents returns and gives a reason to hand over an email for a layout.

One basket, whole build

Average order value on a page like this comes from bundling. The kit list is assembled for them, priced together, and still editable, which beats asking someone to find eight products themselves.

Why the space chooser

The five category chips on the live page name product families, which only help if you already know that a "tile" is what you want. Naming the room lets someone self-select in one click, and it lets you pre-filter 30 products down to six.

Why m² pricing

A 1m × 1m tile at £26.95 and a 1m × 0.5m tile at £26.95 are not the same purchase, and nothing on the page says so. Leading with m² makes tiles comparable, with the per-unit price kept underneath for the basket.

Why the impact bars

Millimetres are the spec, not the benefit. A four-step impact scale ranks the range consistently and gives a reason to trade up, which the current thickness-in-title approach cannot do.

If you only remember one thing

Thickness is about what you drop, not how heavy you lift

A 200kg squat on 15mm rubber is fine. A 60kg bar dropped from the hip on the same floor will crack the slab underneath. Match the floor to the worst impact it will take, zone by zone.

15mm
Nothing gets dropped
Cardio machines, mat work, dumbbells returned to the rack. Protects the slab and cuts noise.
20–30mm
Controlled lowering
Racks, benches, kettlebells set down rather than dropped. The right floor for most of a room.
40mm+ or a platform
Loaded bar hits the floor
Deadlifts, cleans, snatches. Only needed where the bar actually lands, usually 4–8m².

Sells more, not less

Zoning looks like it reduces the order because it puts cheaper tile in most of the room. In practice it adds the 40mm drop zone, the trims and the connectors that a single-product basket never picks up.

Nobody buys one floor

The current page assumes a single product per order. Real gyms mix thicknesses, and the visitor has no way to express that until they are in the basket adding two SKUs and hoping the heights match.

The plan is the lead

"Email me this plan" captures a specified, priced project from someone not ready to buy today. For commercial enquiries that is a better outcome than a cart.
