The maker & region

Domaine de la Janasse

The Sabon family have farmed the southern Rhône for four generations, working from their base in Courthézon on the doorstep of Châteauneuf-du-Pape. Aimé Sabon founded the estate in 1973; today his children Christophe and Isabelle craft wines of remarkable depth and purity, balancing old-vine power with a precise, modern hand in the cellar. This Côtes du Rhône is sourced from old Grenache and Syrah parcels on the same galets-strewn soils as their grander cuvées — generous, gently peppery, and astonishing value for the money.

Côtes du Rhône

The Côtes du Rhône is France's second-largest appellation, sweeping down the river valley from Vienne to Avignon. A warm Mediterranean climate and the famous Mistral wind ripen Grenache, Syrah and Mourvèdre to plush, spice-laden reds. The southern sector — where this wine is grown — is defined by galets roulés, the smooth river stones that store the day's heat and release it through the night, building wines of warmth, body and dark-fruited generosity.

Region guide · France

Wines of Burgundy

The spiritual home of Pinot Noir and Chardonnay — where a single hillside can change everything. Explore 184 Burgundies, from village wines to grand cru.

Few wine regions inspire as much devotion — or confusion — as Burgundy. The good news: once you understand its logic, it becomes one of the most rewarding regions to explore.

Stretching from Chablis in the north to the Mâconnais in the south, Burgundy is built almost entirely on two grapes: Pinot Noir for reds and Chardonnay for whites. What changes is the terroir — the soil, slope and aspect of each individual vineyard, or climat.

That's why Burgundy is classified by place rather than producer, rising from regional Bourgogne up through village, premier cru and grand cru. Our buyers focus on honest, well-made bottles at every level — including growers who over-deliver for the price.

Grand cru is the top tier — single vineyards judged to produce the very best wines. Premier cru sits just below, naming specific high-quality vineyards within a village.
